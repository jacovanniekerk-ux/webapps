Spot the Mistake: Setting Up & Using SwiftGrade

Identify common errors when using SwiftGrade in the classroom

🔎
Read the scenarios below and identify the mistake being made. Click on the best answer.
Scenario 1Spot the Mistake

Ms. V opens SwiftGrade for the first time and goes straight to creating a new assessment. She sets up all her questions, prints the answer sheets, and only then realises the sheets show blank name fields instead of her learners' names.

What is the main mistake in this scenario?

The correct order in SwiftGrade is: create a class → add learners → create the assessment → print answer sheets. When learners are loaded into the class first, SwiftGrade automatically prints each learner's name on their individual answer sheet, making scanning and tracking much more accurate.

You can bulk-add students by pasting a copied list of names directly into SwiftGrade, and use "auto-generate username and password" to save time.
Scenario 2Spot the Mistake

Mr. H hands out SwiftGrade answer sheets for a fill-in-the-blank test. He tells his learners they can write in their normal cursive handwriting and use whatever size writing feels comfortable — "just as long as it fits in the block."

What is the mistake in this scenario?

Cursive writing is much harder for AI to interpret reliably. SwiftGrade is clear about its three writing rules: write big, write in the blocks, write in print (not cursive). When learners follow these rules, the AI's accuracy increases significantly and the number of flagged responses drops.

Display these three rules on the board before every SwiftGrade assessment — a quick 30-second reminder can save you several minutes of manual corrections afterwards.
Scenario 3Spot the Mistake

Ms. O is entering her memo into SwiftGrade for a fill-in-the-blank section. She notices the "auto-correct student spelling errors" toggle but leaves it switched off, thinking her learners will spell the scientific terms perfectly.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

Auto-correct exists precisely because learner spelling is imperfect — especially with scientific or geographic terms. With auto-correct on, SwiftGrade can detect that "cairn" and "Cairn" are the same answer, or that a word written slightly differently is still correct. Turning it off leads to unfair deductions for minor errors.

Keep auto-correct on for fill-in-the-blank questions. You can always manually override a mark if you feel auto-correct was too generous in a specific case.
Scenario 4Spot the Mistake

After scanning his class's scripts, Mr. P sees a class average of 54%. He opens the results, glances at the overall percentage, and tells the class "you need to study harder" before closing the app and moving on to new content.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

The class average tells you how much learners struggled — the analysis tells you exactly where. SwiftGrade flags questions with a bell icon when performance was poor, allowing the teacher to click into each question, see what learners answered, and plan targeted reteaching. Skipping this step breaks the feedback loop completely.

The bell indicators in SwiftGrade's analysis are your reteaching agenda. Any question flagged with a bell is a topic to revisit before your next formal assessment.
