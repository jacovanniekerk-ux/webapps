Spot the Mistake: Video Curation & Setup

Identify common errors when preparing and presenting videos

🔎
Read the scenarios below and identify the mistake being made. Click on the best answer.
Scenario 1Spot the Mistake

Mr. K plays a 12-minute science video from start to finish without pausing, hoping the learners will absorb all the information before he asks questions at the very end.

What is the main mistake in this scenario?

Video should be used as a supplement, not a replacement for the teacher. Playing long videos uninterrupted leads to passive learners.

Pause the video every few minutes to elaborate, ask for hypotheses, or discuss difficult terminology.
Scenario 2Spot the Mistake

Ms. L needs to show a video on conductivity. She opens the YouTube homepage on the classroom projector and starts typing her search query in front of the class, exposing learners to her homepage recommendations and irrelevant thumbnails.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

Opening YouTube's homepage in class is dangerous because the algorithm often suggests irrelevant or distracting videos that pull learners off-task immediately.

Find your videos ahead of time and copy the URLs into a Google Doc or directly into your Google Slides presentation.
Scenario 3Spot the Mistake

Mr. T is projecting a YouTube video. He plays it in the default view, leaving the "Up Next" sidebar and the comments section fully visible on the screen.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

Leaving the sidebars visible causes cognitive overload and split attention. Learners will naturally read the comments or look at the "Up Next" thumbnails instead of focusing on your lesson.

Press 't' on your keyboard to quickly switch to Cinema Mode, or 'f' for Full Screen.
Scenario 4Spot the Mistake

Ms. P embeds an experiment video into her Google Slides. As it plays, she tries to explain the steps to her class, but the loud background music from the video makes it hard for the learners to hear her.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

If the video's audio (like background music or a distracting narrator) isn't necessary, it competes with your voice and hinders understanding.

In Google Slides format options, check the "Mute audio" box so you can comfortably narrate over the visuals without interference.
