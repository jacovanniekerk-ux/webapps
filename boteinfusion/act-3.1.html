Statement 1True or False?

"To maintain engagement, you should keep educational videos between 3 to 6 minutes long and break them up with pauses."

TrueShort forms prevent passive viewing.

Because the modern generation is accustomed to short-form content, their attention span is limited. Keeping a video between three to six minutes and breaking it up with pauses helps learners feel the length of the video less and stay engaged.

Statement 2True or False?

"The YouTube algorithm is primarily designed to educate learners by suggesting relevant academic content."

FalseThe algorithm is designed to distract.

YouTube's algorithm is designed to keep users watching, meaning it easily distracts learners and pulls them down a "rabbit hole" of unrelated content. This is why curating your videos carefully is crucial.

Use an alternate Google account for classroom presentations to prevent your personal interests from appearing in the suggestions!
Statement 3True or False?

"If a video is marked as 'Made for Kids', you can easily save it directly to your YouTube playlists."

FalseThis action is disabled for kids' content.

YouTube disables the "Save to Playlist" and commenting features on videos marked as 'Made for Kids' to protect minors. Because of this restriction, you cannot add them to your standard YouTube playlists.

Instead of using YouTube playlists, save the video URLs directly into a shared Google Doc. This allows easy access and collaboration with colleagues.
Statement 4True or False?

"When inserting a YouTube video into Google Slides, you can set it to automatically stop playing at a specific time."

TrueControl exact playback segments.

By using the formatting options in Google Slides, you can specify both the start and end times for the embedded video. This allows you to show learners bite-sized chunks without needing any external video editing software.
