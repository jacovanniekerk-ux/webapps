Self-Assessment: Add-In vs. Add-On & Learning Events

Test your understanding of e-pedagogy and building a digital lesson

🎯
Are these statements about integrating technology into your lessons true or false? Click to reveal the key concepts and explanations.
Statement 1True or False?

"Technology is genuinely integrated into a lesson when it is used as an add-on — something extra you play if there happens to be time left over at the end of the lesson."

FalseTechnology should be an add-in, not an add-on.

When technology is treated as an extra thing bolted onto the end of a lesson, it never becomes truly integrated into the way we teach. Once technology is added into the learning experience instead, you can no longer argue "there's no time for technology" — because that would be the same as arguing there's no time for teaching.

Ask yourself: is this technology part of how I'm teaching, or is it just something extra I'm hoping to squeeze in?
Statement 2True or False?

"ICT integration should be guided first by the learning outcomes, then by the learning events that support them, and only then by the technology chosen."

TrueOutcomes first, then events, then technology.

The guiding question should always be: what is the learner supposed to learn? Once the outcome is clear, you consider which learning event could lead to it — and only then do you decide which technology could enhance that event.

Reversing this order — technology first — is exactly how technology ends up as an add-on instead of an add-in.
Statement 3True or False?

"The Learning Events framework covered in this module includes five types of activities: acquisition, discovery, dialogue, practice, and creation."

TrueFive events, moving from teacher-led to learner-driven.

Acquisition is narrative, listening, and apprehending — the classic "sage on the stage." Discovery hands learners curated resources to explore on their own. Dialogue involves discussion and collaboration. Practice is applying what's been learned. Creation is producing something new.

A web quest built from the QR codes and Bitly links covered in the previous module is a perfect example of a discovery event.
Statement 4True or False?

"Acquisition — where the teacher stands at the front of the class presenting information — is a learner-centred event, similar to discovery."

FalseAcquisition is teacher-led; discovery is learner-centred.

Acquisition is very much a teacher-driven process — the teacher has the information and disseminates it to the class. Discovery flips this: learners are given resources and allowed to explore and find things on their own, making it a far more learner-centric event.
