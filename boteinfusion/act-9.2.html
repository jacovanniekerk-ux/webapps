Spot the Mistake: Navigating the WCED ePortal

Identify common errors when finding, saving and using ePortal resources

🔎
Read the scenarios below and identify the mistake being made. Click on the best answer.
Scenario 1Spot the Mistake

Mr. K wants grade 12 Accounting past papers. He goes straight to the search bar, types "accounting", and is met with tens of thousands of results from every phase and subject. He gives up scrolling after a few minutes, without ever touching the filter panel on the side.

What is the main mistake in this scenario?

Before typing anything into the search bar, first ask what you're looking for and apply filters. Selecting a phase like FET can take a result set from 36,000 down to 21,000, and stacking on grade and subject filters narrows it further still — down to a few hundred relevant resources.

Filter first, search second: phase → grade → subject, then use the search bar only to fine-tune within what's left.
Scenario 2Spot the Mistake

Mr. B finds a useful worksheet on the ePortal and downloads it to his laptop the day it's published. Months later, WCED refreshes that same resource with updated content, but Mr. B keeps using his old downloaded copy, assuming it must already reflect the latest version.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

Downloading turns a resource into static content on the device — a snapshot in time. If Mr. B had instead bookmarked and accessed the resource in its dynamic, online form, the underlying file could be swapped out in future, and he'd simply see the newest version loaded into the same space.

For content that might change (revision resources, updated exemplars), prefer the live link over a permanent download.
Scenario 3Spot the Mistake

Ms. V has a favourite intermediate-phase Afrikaans resource she uses every term. Each time she needs it, she re-applies all her filters and browses back through the collection pages from scratch to find it again.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

The Add to Library button exists precisely so teachers don't have to continually re-browse and re-search the ePortal. Once a resource is added, it sits in "My Library" for quick, repeated access — and can be removed just as easily if it's no longer needed.

Build your library once at the start of a term, then draw on it lesson after lesson.
Scenario 4Spot the Mistake

Ms. R wants to sign up for an account on the ePortal so she can save resources to a library. When she goes to create a profile, she only sees the option for a "learner profile," so she concludes profiles aren't available to staff yet and abandons signing up altogether.

What is the mistake in this scenario?

Currently, the only profile option on the ePortal is a learner profile; a teacher profile with similar but slightly different functionality is planned for 2027. Teachers are still encouraged to create a learner profile now — it centralises saved resources and doubles as a preview of how learners will experience the same site.

Signing up just needs a name, surname, email and password, followed by an email verification link.
